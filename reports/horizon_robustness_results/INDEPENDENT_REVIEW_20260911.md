# Independent review and research-direction decision

11 September 2026. Scope: independent examination of the delegated E2/R1 work,
not a new empirical campaign or a manuscript. Original artifacts, model code,
protocol and submitted articles were not changed.

## Decision

**Retain the undated, order-horizon research direction, but revise the interpretation
and finish a focused validation/comparison phase before writing the full paper.**

The formulation is coherent without dates, the integer representation is
mathematically appropriate, and the screening contains real improvements over
nominal optimization. The industrial activation result deserves further work.
However, the experiment has not established a generally reliable future-feasibility
method, nor established the pivotal claim that its useful protection cannot be
matched by ordinary tightening. Several reporting defects also survived the
delegated review. This is a successful implementation and informative, mixed
screening study—not yet a completed positive validation of the proposed method.

Dates are not the missing ingredient identified by these results. The observed
limitations concern uncertainty-set coverage, comparisons, replication, and
retrospective metadata. Dates alone would not resolve any of those.

The review read the delegated handoff, pilot findings, predeclaration, mathematical

Protected-source checks passed for **92/92 files**. The shared industrial loader

`0c0c38f7b76ba8d41ef156649919b74642fd4c6f1ed45c0eab8f061242ad4142`.
It is not itself listed among those 92 protected files.

Campaign certificate/accounting and recorded-authorization checks passed. This
means recorded manifests, charges and results agree; it does not independently
authenticate a user's historical approval or turn configured caps into measured

**Result: all 318 accepted non-superseded training layouts, 318 primary future

Reproducible audit code, machine-readable results and verification fingerprints

## 2. The screening result, without pilot contamination

| Arm | Scored layouts / 90 authorized cells | Joint passes | Passes / scored | Returned-and-passed / authorized |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NOM | 90/90 | 7 | 7.8% | 7.8% |
| TIGHT, lambda=0.5 | 90/90 | 30 | 33.3% | 33.3% |
| HIST | 66/90 | 34 | 51.5% | 37.8% |
| HIST+ACT | 66/90 | 36 | 54.5% | 40.0% |

These are descriptive counts across correlated horizons, not independent trial
probabilities. The last column is a computationally qualified delivery outcome;
it must not label solver non-returns as observed future infeasibility. Conditional
rates alone select the easier returned cases and overstate practical availability.
Report both, with explicit status categories and horizon/family breakdowns.

All 48 non-returns concern the 50-product family: each robust arm returns 12/36
layouts there. No experiment established infeasibility. In particular, two reported
minimum-slack intervals straddle delta=0.01 and remain unresolved.

On the 2000-product family, TIGHT passes 9/9 and HIST/HIST+ACT 7/9. On the
1000-product family, TIGHT passes 7/12 and HIST 10/12. Thus even the synthetic
evidence is not simply “tightening always suffices” or “the hull always helps.”

## 3. Industrial result and an informative model failure

| Horizon n | NOM worst excess (pp) | TIGHT | HIST | HIST+ACT | HIST+ACT joint pass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 10,937 | 2.3655 | 1.2390 | 0.4312 | 0 | Yes |
| 21,874 | 2.7482 | 1.1289 | 0.3057 | 0.0890 | No |
| 43,748 | 2.2341 | 0.8670 | 0.3542 | 0 | Yes |

All other arms and the frozen reference fail joint compliance at all three
horizons. HIST+ACT's achieved future visits per order are 2.7836, 2.8981 and
2.9824. At the two passing horizons these are about 10.9% and 12.9% below the
reference's 3.1251 and 3.4248. These are valid comparisons of achieved layouts,
not statements about optimality. The reference comparison remains context, not
a replacement for the NOM/TIGHT comparisons.

The primary n=P result is a real failure, not a rounding error. Its violating
station is **S20** (artifact label `S_20`):

- historical target: 2.9547303%;
- allowed ceiling: 3.9547303%;
- modelled worst share: 3.9246187%;
- realized future share: 4.0437392%;
- historically inactive products at that station: **zero**;
- frozen-product workload at that station in this future: **zero**.

The excess is 0.0890089 percentage points. Because the realized share exceeds
the modelled station envelope, this future is demonstrably outside the declared
uncertainty set in a relevant direction. The failure concerns variation among
historically active products. Merely observing total activation below nu does
not establish uncertainty-set coverage. At the passing horizons, no station
envelope exceedance was detected, but that does not prove full product-vector
membership in the uncertainty set.

The industrial catalogue has 715 historically inactive products: **616 fixed
and 99 movable**. Fixed inactive products occupy 11 stations. The ACT layouts
place inactive products in 12, 13 and 14 stations across the three horizons,
versus 17, 18 and 18 for HIST. The number actually receiving future orders is
73, 181 and 455—not 181 at every horizon.

For a station necessarily holding an inactive product, the activation row is
equivalent to the scenario restriction

    A_sk <= min(u_s, (u_s - nu)/(1 - nu)),  for nu < 1.

At delta=nu=0.01 without cap clipping, its active-history ceiling is b_s/0.99.
Thus ACT provides both scenario-dependent margin reservation and decisions about
the movable inactive products. Its benefit cannot yet be attributed exclusively
to successful prediction of activation. Stronger ordinary tightening and,
potentially, a separately declared station-specific margin control are important
competing explanations. The latter would be a new exploratory comparison, not
an unannounced change to the primary protocol.

Upper-only success is also not symmetric preservation. The passing ACT layouts
still have maximum station-share decreases of about **1.67 pp and 2.66 pp** at
the short and long horizons. These are permitted by the approved policy. If the
intended business requirement remains “every station stays within plus/minus
one point,” that is a different policy and must be discussed explicitly.

## 4. Independent verdict on H1–H4 and claims C1–C5

| Hypothesis / delegated claim | My assessment |
|---|---|
| H1 / C1: historical protection improves future compliance over NOM | Supported descriptively by this screening, conditional on the recorded solve outcomes. Every available family-by-horizon paired robust mean worst excess is lower than NOM's. Not a general probability guarantee or a claim about every instance. |
| H2 / C2: gains are not explained by ordinary tightening at comparable cost | **Not established.** The lambda=0.5 comparison is informative and mixed, but one tightening level is not a frontier. The industrial signal merits follow-up; broad incremental value remains open. |
| C3: a pre-campaign drift prediction was independently confirmed | **Reject this characterization.** Keep the noise comparison only as an exploratory descriptive heuristic. It neither establishes drift nor independently validates a prediction. |
| H3 / C4: activation protection is useful | Promising industrial case evidence, not general validation. Testable at this one origin; not testable on the synthetic cases because HIST and ACT coincide there. The failed primary industrial horizon and margin-reservation alternative matter. |
| H4 / C5: horizon changes the trade-off | Mathematically and descriptively supported in the limited sense of the original H4. Nested uncertainty sets explain model-side relaxation. A general claim that larger n improves future compliance is unsupported. |

Reasons for rejecting C3's stronger interpretation:

1. The append log records SCREENING_LAUNCHED, then SLACK_SURVEY_COMPLETE, then
   DRIFT_SIGNATURE_FOUND (lines 45–47). It does not support the claimed
   pre-campaign chronology. Pilot future results were already known. The original
   plan also explicitly calls this retrospective/exploratory because these data
   had been inspected before.
2. The noise scale uses uniform station share 1/S and treats workload lines as
   multinomial trials. Products within an order are correlated, orders may be
   dependent, actual station shares are unequal, and the reported numerator is
   a maximum over stations/blocks. A ratio to that crude single-share standard
   deviation is not a calibrated drift test. Stationary dependent demand could
   also produce persistence relative to that benchmark.
3. The numerator is one reference layout's required slack—an upper bound on
   the minimum—not the optimum required slack. The same warehouse/history underlies
   the heuristic and subsequent results; the campaign is not independent evidence
   of the proposed causal explanation.

H4 needs a denominator correction too. HIST conditional compliance is
9/18, 11/18, 14/30 across the three horizons; ACT is 10/18, 11/18, 15/30.
Neither rises monotonically. Passes over the fixed 30 authorized cells do rise,
partly because more layouts return. Those statements must not be conflated.
Nor does nesting require independently found time-limited incumbent upper bounds
to be monotone; monotonicity applies to the exact optimum, or to a common candidate
pool evaluated under nested sets.

## 5. Defects and unsupported judgments to correct before writing

### P0: Separate campaigns in all publication outputs

The delegated fix to campaign-aware tabular grouping did not reach the figures.
`make_analysis.py:71` combines pilot and screen, then passes the combined frame to
the plots at line 123 onward. `figures.py:88` groups without campaign identity.
The current compliance plot has **128 rows at n=P**, including eight pilot rows,
instead of the screening's 120. This mixes formulations and duplicates conditions.
Use screen-only publication figures; place the pilot in an explicitly separate
engineering appendix. Also preserve campaign/version identity in secondary,
station-profile and resource tables. The present cross-horizon table drops it.

`analysis.py:451` labels negative differences “better” for every metric, including
joint_pass. That direction is reversed for a success metric. Correct the metric
directions and add negative regression controls. Numerical paired means themselves
are not changed by this labeling defect.

The cross-horizon plot calculates cell-count labels but never displays them; its
legend takes handles only from the first panel, which need not contain every arm.
Its absolute-n grouping also merges unlike catalogue-relative horizons. Repair
denominators, identities and grouping before publication.

### P0: Restore diagnostic evidence or downgrade its claims

`min_slack_diagnostic.py:59` saves a validity flag and summarized bounds; it never
saves `result.assignment`, the full SolveResult, or the full validation certificate.
The diagnostic JSON and CSV contain no witness layouts. A self-hash certifies
the summary's bytes, not its mathematical correctness. The BERNER recheck likewise
saves a layout hash rather than the layout.

Search for recoverable native artifacts first. If absent, the exact six diagnostic
bounds cannot be independently replayed from this package. Describe them as logged
diagnostic results, or rerun explicitly authorized diagnostics with full artifacts.
Do not claim this audit independently certified their precise bounds. Existing
screening layouts independently establish attainability in their solved cases,
including all three BERNER ACT models; that conclusion need not rely on the
unreplayable recheck. The two small unresolved cases remain unresolved.

Harden the diagnostic path before reuse: require successful structural/validation
checks before accepting an upper bound, check bound consistency and numerical
provenance, and retain witnesses. Currently the verdict logic uses the upper
bound even if `layout_valid` were false (the saved six flags happen to be true).

### P1: Correct scientific overstatements and transcriptions

- The handoff's H2 table labels the BERNER reductions **HIST minus TIGHT**, but
  1.239/1.040/0.867 pp are the **ACT** reductions. HIST's reductions are
  0.808/0.823/0.513 pp. Do not attribute the combined model's benefit to the hull alone.
- Cross-horizon evaluations reuse layouts and overlapping futures. They are
  secondary transfer checks, not independent replication. There are 624 screening
  evaluations, plus 12 pilot evaluations; the 636 combined total is not a new sample.
- A 0.522% training-objective inversion demonstrates suboptimality of a particular
  incumbent. It is not an estimated universal solver-noise floor or an upper bound
  on optimization error. Achieved future visit differences remain real; neither
  their sign nor their magnitude establishes a difference between exact optimal
  objective values. Future objective ordering is not implied by training feasible-set
  nesting, even at exact training optimality.
- “Compliance is unaffected by solver non-optimality” is true only for the accuracy
  of scoring a fixed returned layout. Which layout the algorithm returns—and hence
  its future compliance—can change with seed or computational effort.
- `PILOT_FINDINGS.md` still contains the obsolete 6,999.4-second elapsed figure
  in its resource table, despite a corrected 8,073.75 figure elsewhere.
- Its noise-scale table has a factor-of-ten conversion error. For example,
  2.5432 pp is 0.025432 share units, not 0.00254; 0.0826 pp is 0.000826,
  not 0.0000826. The ratios reported in the CSV-based calculation use the
  proper pp comparison; the prose table does not.
- Stage D was not executed. The predeclaration contemplated residual-budget
  frontiers, while the final log says they were never authorized. Reconcile that
  scope history; do not say the budget was exhausted or silently launch a frontier.
- The failed pilot's 6,840 seconds are configured/charged caps, **not actual native
  time spent**: its saved native result reports about 119.65 seconds, and the entire
  invocation lasted 425.44 seconds. Screening's configured caps total 66,600 seconds;
  saved native solve times total about 66,602.86 seconds and build times 3,928.22
  seconds. Keep cap charges, solve measurements and elapsed time distinct.
- The claimed 2.93 GB overall memory maximum is stale pilot evidence. The screening
  case frame reports a maximum of about 3.325 GiB, still well below 32 GiB.
- Byte-scanning compressed PNG/PDF files is not a valid rendered-text anonymization
  check. The provided checker passed its text detector, but its claim to inspect
  figure text is too strong. Check plotting labels and rendered outputs explicitly.

### P1: Preserve the correct mathematical/numerical boundary

The integer floor argument is correct: for integer z, z<=r iff z<=floor(r).
The activation endpoint and clipped-cap/nu=1 cases agree with the original exact
constraints, and enumeration/native tests pass. Retain this representation and
the independent exact validator; no safety margin or enlarged evaluation tolerance
is justified by the evidence.

However, “integer coefficients plus tolerance below one makes any solver exact”
is too broad. A finite-precision MIP solver also has integrality errors, scaling,
and coefficient representation limits. Int64 safety is not the same as exact
float64 representation in CPLEX. These are reasons to retain post-validation,
not evidence that this screening is invalid. IBM explicitly documents the
finite-precision and tolerance qualifications of CPLEX feasibility:
[IBM numerical limitations](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/icos/22.1.2?topic=solutions-limitations-due-numeric-difficulties).

## 6. What remains scientifically justified without dates

The deployment horizon is **the next n complete orders**, with historical scenario
blocks of the same n. A longer learning prefix supplies several such blocks; it
is not an inconsistent choice of prediction horizon. Common workload rescaling
leaves shares unchanged under the user's abstract linear-scaling assumption.

The closed catalogue is essential: all 21,874 retained industrial products have
slots, including the historically inactive ones. The 5,899 frozen products remain
in workload/visits; their future demand is recomputed. The two excluded stations
and their products do not enter the denominator. The loader reconstructs this
snapshot from the whole export by explicit amendment, so these are results
conditional on assumed pre-known metadata—not proof of a prospective deployment.

The guarantee is conditional: a model-feasible layout respects its ceilings for
every demand vector inside the declared set. No established statement assigns
a probability to future set membership, covers arbitrary future mixes, protects
every prefix, or selects a universally correct n. Dates would not make those
stronger guarantees follow automatically.

Keep the historical target b. Its derivation from the reference is the stated
policy, not a reason to reset targets or discard the study. Reference headroom
and optimization's use of slack are precisely why the tightening control matters.
Do not change b or delta after seeing future outcomes.

## 7. Prioritized next work and decision rules

### First: evidence/reporting repair, no empirical solver budget

1. Correct campaign isolation, metric directions, figure denominators and the
   identified narrative/table errors. Add regression tests and regenerate a
   separate screen-only report; retain superseded originals.
2. Reconcile/recover diagnostic witnesses and resource authorization records.
   Explicitly downgrade anything not recoverable rather than reconstructing a
   purported original result from a later run.
3. Document the S20 active-demand failure and fixed/movable inactive-product
   decomposition. Preserve all future failures and the original primary settings.

### Second: a bounded, predeclared follow-up campaign

There are about **9.12 configured solver-hours** left under the reported B–F cap
accounting (108,000-75,180 seconds). Confirm permission and obtain a new explicit
manifest/quote before using them. Do not equate that allowance with end-to-end
wall time. My priority is diagnostic value, not filling the remaining budget.

1. **Complete the tightening comparison first.** On the already scoped lowest-seed
   50/500 families and full BERNER, use the declared lambda grid and unchanged
   ceilings. NOM/lambda=0 and lambda=0.5 already exist. Three additional
   horizon-independent nominal solves per dataset would cost about 6,660 configured
   seconds (1.85 h) at existing caps, if model reuse remains valid. Score their
   frozen layouts at all three declared horizons. Do not select a winning lambda
   from these futures and call it a deployable policy.
2. **Study nu and delta sensitivity, retaining n=P as a declared focal horizon**
   for any reduced industrial frontier, rather than selecting one of its two
   passing horizons. The four missing nu values on BERNER at n=P would cost
   about 2 h at current caps; nu=0 and nu=0.01 already have matching models.
   Synthetic nu runs are redundant at this origin. Quote the delta comparisons
   separately, since a full all-arm/all-horizon frontier will exceed a small
   remaining allowance. A reduced frontier must be labelled as reduced and cannot
   be represented as completion of the full sensitivity plan.
3. **Recover or rerun only diagnostics needed for a decision**, with full stored
   solutions and certificates. Do not spend the balance merely trying to force
   an infeasibility proof in the two unresolved small cases. Additional diagnostic
   search after observing difficulty can be legitimate exploratory work if
   transparently labelled and budgeted; it is not automatically scientifically
   invalid. The original stopped runs must remain intact.

If the margin controls dominate ACT at comparable achieved visit cost and effort,
report that negative result for added model complexity. A simpler tightening policy
may then be preferable. If the benefit persists, it supports retaining the model,
but it still needs a stronger external-validity check before a broad positive claim.

### Third: fresh authority for replication or model revision

At least one further predeclared solver seed on the same cells would address
algorithm variability; another eligible origin would address a different future
segment. These are different questions and neither gives independent warehouses.
Do not count overlapping horizons as a substitute. The full industrial export
does not support a second disjoint 2P future starting after the first 2P future;
smaller later horizons would be a separately declared, partial replication.

If active-demand envelope failures persist, discuss a bounded full-catalogue
composition-transfer extension rather than automatically raising nu or delta.
That changes the uncertainty family and requires a new exploratory protocol,
training-only construction and unchanged future evaluation rules. The present S20
failure motivates investigating this possibility; it does not yet establish which
alternative model will work.

Return to dates if the intended question becomes shift/day/week compliance,
arrival rates or service capacity, or if order IDs cannot support credible
processing chronology. Do not switch merely to obtain a more positive result.
Actual pre-cutoff inventory metadata, if available, would address snapshot
conditioning more directly than dates on order rows alone.

## Bottom line

**Do not discard this study, and do not yet write it as a validated general
robustness solution.** Preserve the undated framework, correct the evidence
presentation, resolve the tightening/activation comparison and sensitivity gap,
and make the next decision using the original ceilings. A mixed or negative
comparison can still be an honest research outcome; publishability and novelty
remain separate questions requiring literature positioning and joint review.
