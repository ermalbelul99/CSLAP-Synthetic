# Independent decision after exploratory revision 2

Date: 13 September 2026. Scope: independent examination and research-direction
decision, not manuscript writing, protocol revision or a new empirical campaign.
This review supersedes the 11 September review's forward-looking experimental
recommendations where the user subsequently approved different work.

## 1. Decision

**The authorized exploratory experiments are finished. The originally hoped-for
positive result—a demonstrated advantage of the proposed scenario/activation
method in preserving future workload shares—is not established. We are not yet
at the finish line for that positive robustness paper.**

Retain the undated, next-n-orders formulation. The evidence does not identify
missing calendar dates as the cause of the failures. Do not declare HIST+ACT the
winning method, and do not automatically buy another large parameter search.
TIGHT is the strongest practical candidate in the new industrial comparison,
but remains a candidate, not a validated general deployment policy.

There is enough evidence to close this exploratory comparison with a mixed or
negative conclusion about added model complexity. After the bounded correctness
and reporting repairs below, such a study can be written honestly without
requiring every original hypothesis to be positive. Whether that narrower
contribution is sufficiently novel for a particular journal remains a separate
literature-positioning decision, not something passing software tests decides.

My preferred next decision is: fix the identified defects first; if the desired
paper must make a positive practical-method claim, authorize one bounded,
fixed-policy validation campaign on a previously unscored future segment. Do not
continue tuning against the current industrial futures until they all pass.

## 3. What the current results actually establish

At the revised primary policy, every retained station is scored within plus or
minus **2 percentage points** of its frozen historical target. The targets are
not replaced by the incumbent's future shares.

| Arm | BERNER primary passes, delta=0.02 | Four-instance synthetic screen, delta=0.02 |
|---|---:|---:|
| NOM | 0/3 | 6/12 |
| TIGHT, lambda=0.5 | 3/3 | 10/12 |
| HIST | 0/3 | 9/11 scored; one additional no-allocation case |
| HIST+ACT | 1/3 | 9/11 scored; the same no-allocation case |
| Incumbent, context only | 3/3 | Not the optimized-method comparator |

The industrial sensitivity table is also correct: TIGHT passes 1/3 at delta=.01
and 3/3 at .03; HIST passes none at either; HIST+ACT passes none at either.

For primary TIGHT, the maximum absolute station deviations on the three futures
are 1.3363, 1.5582 and 1.4727 percentage points. Its visit reductions versus the
incumbent are respectively 13.52%, 13.71% and 14.24%. These are useful achieved
layout properties, not optimality or population-level claims.

Across the three nested industrial windows, the descriptive mean visits/order
is 2.8250 for TIGHT, 2.8034 for HIST and 2.8040 for HIST+ACT. TIGHT costs about
0.77% more than HIST in this comparison. Do not call this an identified optimal
price of robustness: all are time-limited returned layouts. Indeed, at n=P/2,
TIGHT has fewer visits than HIST; the average is not a uniform per-window cost.

HIST's largest primary breach is 0.4088 percentage points; HIST+ACT's is 0.2515.
These are smaller failures than NOM's worst 0.4507, but they remain failures of
the declared policy. A 0.4088-point breach uses about another 20.4% beyond the
2-point allowance; its practical importance is not settled by calling it tiny.
At delta=.01, the displayed HIST+ACT '0.00 pp' miss is actually 0.0044179 pp.
Use sufficient precision to distinguish such misses from exact compliance.

### The main hypotheses

The original H1–H4 were upper-only hypotheses. Their two-sided analogues are an
exploratory amendment, not grounds to overwrite the earlier experiment.

| Question | Current reading |
|---|---|
| H1: do historical scenarios improve on NOM? | Mixed support: often smaller breaches and better synthetic compliance, but HIST still fails every primary industrial horizon. There is no universal improvement claim. |
| H2: is the gain more than ordinary tightening? | Not established. At the primary industrial policy, ordinary TIGHT is the strongest compliance competitor at similar achieved visit cost. This is the decisive missing positive result for the more complex model. |
| H3: does inactive-product activation add useful protection? | Not established as a general benefit. HIST+ACT improves some industrial outcomes but not consistently enough to win. All four revision-2 synthetic instances have empty inactive sets, so HIST and HIST+ACT are identical there, not independent evidence for activation. |
| H4: does the horizon matter? | Descriptively yes. Larger n is not uniformly better. Three overlapping horizons do not determine a universal n or a reliability curve. |

This does not prove HIST+ACT is intrinsically inferior under every parameter,
solver, instance or uncertainty family. It does mean we cannot market its added
complexity as empirically justified by the current two-sided comparison.

## 4. What remains valid without dates

The information contract is coherent: reliable order sequence, a known closed
catalogue, one allocation fixed before scoring, a historical reference target,
and a future horizon of the next n complete orders. Historical scenario blocks
have the same n; a longer learning prefix supplies multiple such blocks.

All 21,874 retained industrial products receive slots, including 715 with zero
historical orders. The 5,899 frozen products contribute their actual future
workload and visits. The 24-station retained system excludes the two small
stations from both numerator and denominator. The full-export warehouse snapshot
is assumed pre-known under the explicit industrial amendment; this remains a
retrospective, snapshot-conditioned study, not a demonstrated reconstruction of
metadata available at a real historical deployment.

Common proportional scaling of workload leaves shares unchanged. Changing the
demand mix or extending a window with different orders does not. Dates would
help define shifts/days/weeks and examine seasonality, but do not themselves
choose the right horizon, calibrate a future uncertainty set, establish the
inventory information boundary, or guarantee an arbitrary future mix.

For the declared set, robust feasibility remains a conditional statement:
if future demand belongs to that set, the certified layout respects the band.
There is no estimated probability here that the next demand vector belongs to
the set. Statistical guarantees require their own construction and assumptions;
they do not follow merely from having historical scenarios. For an example of
an explicit statistical construction, see [Bertsimas, Gupta and Kallus,
Data-Driven Robust Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0212). That paper's
guarantee is not being imported into this chronological integer-assignment study.

### A useful interpretation of TIGHT

TIGHT is not the opposite of robustness. Let qH be pooled historical product
shares and rho=lambda*delta. For any station subset of products,

    |r_s(x,q)-r_s(x,qH)| <= TV(q,qH),
    TV(q,qH) = (1/2) sum_p |q_p-qH_p|.

Therefore the triangle inequality gives

    |r_s(x,qH)-b_s| <= delta-rho,  TV(q,qH) <= rho
    implies |r_s(x,q)-b_s| <= delta.

Clipping the bands at zero and one does not change this implication. At the
primary setting, TIGHT reserves rho=.01 in share units. This is a simple
conditional robust interpretation, obtainable without dates; it is not a novel
general theorem, nor proof that these futures lie in that total-variation ball.
The original plan already discusses the upper-only version of this control.

The strongest useful research direction may consequently be *how to select and
validate protective margin*, not *how to make HIST+ACT win*. A comparison that
finds a simple robust control sufficient can be informative.

## 5. Why the positive finish-line claim is premature

1. **No independent industrial replication.** TIGHT and NOM each use one unique
   industrial layout per delta, reused at all three horizons. Their six secondary
   transfer scores repeat each of the same three layout/future evaluations twice.
   They are not six new tests. There is one origin and one optimizer seed.
2. **Limited benchmark coverage under the final rule.** The old upper-only screen
   covered 29 synthetic instances; the two-sided revision covers four, one per
   size family. These are not a complete two-sided rerun of the 29-instance
   benchmark. Stationary synthetic data cannot supply the missing industrial
   regime-shift replication.
3. **Exploration, not untouched confirmation.** The two-sided policy was specified
   before its solves, but after examining the same future demand through earlier
   upper-only layouts and two-sided rescoring. Prefix-only model fitting is
   verified; independent confirmatory evaluation of the chosen revision is not.
4. **Delta=.02 is a defensible study convention, not an established business
   acceptance threshold.** Incumbent historical dispersion can motivate the grid.
   It cannot prove a smaller allowance impossible for another allocation, choose
   an operationally acceptable slack by itself, or supply a future coverage level.
5. **No fair frontier-based dominance conclusion.** Lambda and nu frontiers were
   not run. Lambda was explicitly declined, so I am not treating it as unfinished
   authorized execution. Its absence limits claims about method-wide dominance,
   not our ability to report the fixed lambda=.5 comparison honestly.
6. **Future uncertainty mismatch is observed, not solved.** At primary n=P,
   HIST+ACT has zero upward envelope departures but five downward departures,
   including two floor breaches. At n=P/2 it has two downward departures; at 2P,
   seven. These are direct witnesses that the actual future is outside the
   modeled set in those station directions, even in the passing 2P case.
   The current `station_novelty_count` measures only the upper direction.

No recorded lower bound establishes that the desired band is unattainable.
Failing on an unseen future, or returning no incumbent within a time cap, is not
an impossibility proof. Conversely, no proof of impossibility is required to
conclude that this particular experimental method has not delivered its hoped-for
positive comparative result.

## 6. Corrections required before general-method claims or publication

### 6.1 General lower-activation formula is overly conservative

The revision assumes activation can always be placed outside station s, giving
the lower endpoint (1-nu)*A_sk. This is false when every inactive product belongs
to s. For nonempty Z define

    g_s = min_{p in Z} x_ps = 1 if all inactive products are at s, else 0.

The true minimum is

    min_k min{ A_sk, (1-nu)*A_sk + nu*g_s }.

The lower activation integer row must correspond to

    (M-N)*C_sk + N*T_k*g_s >= ceil(M*f_s*T_k).

The missing term appears in `uncertainty.py::integer_cap_rows`, the validator's
lower envelope, and both minimum-slack backends. Existing tests compare these
implementations to one another or use empty inactive sets; they do not provide
an independent general lower-vertex oracle.

I constructed a solver-free four-product counterexample. Targets are (.90,.10),
delta=.01, nu=.05, and the sole inactive product is assigned to station 0. The
true station ranges are [.900,.905] and [.095,.100], entirely feasible. The
validator instead invents a .855 lower share for station 0 and rejects the layout
by .035. See `edge_case.py` and `edge_case_result.json`.

**This does not invalidate the recorded campaigns.** The raw audit establishes
that each synthetic inactive set is empty, while BERNER has frozen inactive
products at 11 stations. Thus every feasible industrial layout has an inactive
product outside every given station. The omitted term is zero for all feasible
layouts of these empirical models, not merely for the layouts that happened to
be returned. Fix/generalize the implementation and proof with an independent
vertex or LP oracle; preserve the old artifacts and document this zero-impact
argument. No rerun of the 44 solves is justified solely by this edge case.

### 6.2 Secondary consistency defects

- `minimum_slack_for_assignment` still computes only upward excursions even for
  a two-sided problem. A six-product, three-station counterexample gives true
  minimum slack .20 while the helper returns .10. It is also used for CPLEX
  minimum-slack warm starts. Revision 2 ran visits mode, so its reported outcomes
  do not depend on this helper. Correct it before using two-sided diagnostics.
- Two-sided novelty reporting needs lower as well as upper envelope departures.
  The raw audit above supplies the missing lower-direction evidence. Absence of
  either type alone is not full product-vector membership certification.
- Upper-only input/model hashes are preserved, but not every live metric's
  behavior: `metrics.py` includes artificial zero floor residuals when computing
  upper-only maximum residual and overall borderline status. A strictly interior
  upper-only case can consequently be labeled borderline with residual zero.
  Stored earlier artifacts remain intact; avoid a blanket byte-identical behavior
  claim and add an upper-only scoring regression check.

### 6.3 Reporting repairs

- `figures.frontier` groups only by arm. The pooled two-sided delta figure thus
  connects distinct datasets and horizons at the same x value, mixing four
  synthetic datasets into delta=.02 but not the other deltas. Its vertical lines
  and connecting paths are not a valid response curve. The CSV keeps the correct
  keys. Facet a matched BERNER comparison by horizon or define an explicit
  descriptive aggregation with its denominator. Per-campaign one-delta figures
  should not be presented as frontiers.
- Handoff section 12.7 says 27 BERNER and 60 synthetic cells. Correct totals are
  **36 BERNER plus 48 synthetic = 84**. Section 12.5's primary misses are not all
  floor breaches: HIST at n=P/2 also breaches an upper cap.
- Replace 'only TIGHT carries a margin' with 'only TIGHT explicitly reserves the
  uniform pooled-history half-band used by this control.' HIST and ACT can also
  create directional headroom. The experiments are consistent with a margin
  mechanism, not a causal isolation of it from solver/layout effects.
- The dispersion code measures deviation from pooled history, not differences
  between consecutive blocks. Incumbent historical failure does not predict
  necessary future failure, nor a lower bound over alternative layouts.
- Keep revision 2 explicitly exploratory. The stale opening statement 'No
  scientific parameter changed' needs an upper-only scope qualifier; the rule and
  primary delta did change in the authorized revision. The old reviewer brief is
  not current protocol authority and retains withdrawn assertions.
- The ledger downgrades the six original missing-witness diagnostics to logged,
  non-replayable results, and the diagnostic tool now saves future witnesses.
  However, the handoff/predeclaration still present the old 'four constructively
  resolved' claims without that qualification. Propagate the qualification into
  the current narrative and table metadata. Do not invent missing original
  witnesses, and do not rerun diagnostics merely to make an old log replayable.

The prior campaign-isolation and metric-direction fixes are in place and pass
their checks. Those are genuine improvements; the new findings are not a reason
to discard the entire evidence package.

## 7. Bounded next work and stopping rule

1. Correct the general lower counterpart/helper and add independent oracle and
   upper-only regression tests. Preserve evidence identities and the documented
   empirical zero-impact argument.
2. Correct the frontier figure, diagnostic qualifications, counts, exploration

3. Agree which claim the paper is intended to make: a completed mixed comparative
   investigation, or a positive validated margin policy. Do not treat these as
   the same finish line.

### My recommendation if a positive practical-method claim is still wanted

Freeze the current primary band (.02), TIGHT lambda=.5, the baseline arms and a
focal horizon **n=P** before examining another future. Use one new eligible
industrial origin and a small predeclared seed set, with fixed budget and no
result-dependent retries. Seeds assess optimizer variation; origins assess a
different demand segment. They cannot substitute for each other.

The retained stream has 284,862 orders. The furthest currently scored future ends
at index 243,151 (half-open). A hypothetical new deployment there has room for
the next P=21,874 orders through 265,025, but not a fresh disjoint 2P window.
Verify that this segment has not been examined in other exploratory analyses
before calling it held out. For the new deployment compute its target from its
own history, then freeze it; do not reset targets during that deployment's future.
The full-export snapshot assumption continues to apply.

This is a proposal requiring new user approval and a quoted manifest, not an
authorized task or a claim that the original ~36-hour allowance has spare time.
The purpose is to challenge the already chosen margin policy, not select a lambda
from its validation outcomes. A broad lambda frontier is not my first purchase now.

Predeclare the evidentiary endpoint as replication or failure of the fixed-policy
finding. If it does not replicate, close the current model family as a mixed or
negative result and discuss a separately justified active-product drift model
only if a revised research question is wanted. Do not keep widening delta or
retuning lambda on the same futures. Even replication would support a bounded
case-study claim, not guarantee all future horizons or all warehouses.

### Dated alternative

Return to dates if the operational question becomes shift/day/week compliance,
calendar seasonality, rates or physical throughput, or if order-ID chronology is
not credible. The present evidence does not justify switching just because the
more complex undated arm failed to beat tightening. Calendar timestamps alone
would not repair that comparison or its uncertainty-set mismatch.

## Bottom line

**No, we have not finished the positive generalized robustness study originally
hoped for. Yes, we have completed a useful exploratory study and have a defensible
decision: keep the undated framework, do not promote HIST+ACT as validated, and
treat margin-based protection as the leading candidate.** Fix the bounded defects,
then either write the qualified mixed-result study or buy the one locked-policy
validation step needed for the stronger claim. No manuscript has been started.
