Q-013  raised by: general-purpose/sonnet check author, seq 25 (card "Q-TAIL-UNUSED-SCHEMA")  phase: P1  date: 2026-09-14

**Class:** E. The specification's own rule decides it once one field is defined.
**Status:** RESOLVED

## Question

How is the derived document value `prov.tail_unused` computed and checked? Before this card, `W/evidence/DOCUMENT_VALUES_SPEC.md` gave two formulas: `stream_end − tail_start` and `stream_end + 1 − tail_start`. It said the choice depends on whether `stream_end` is a length or a last index, and that "the extractor must state" which, but it defined no field for that statement. The check author therefore accepted either formula.

## Why it matters

Accepting either formula lets a one-off error pass the P1:G2 check. The unused-tail count is a disclosed number: plan P1 step 3 gives 284,862 − 265,025 = 19,837.

## Decisive test

The source's own wording shows whether 284,862 is a stream length or the index of the last retained order. The two blind extractors must quote that wording.

## Options

- (a) Keep accepting either formula. This is what the author implemented.
- (b) Require a `semantics` field on `prov.stream_end` (`length` or `last_index`). The two extractors must agree on it, and exactly the matching formula is valid.

## Resolution

Resolution: (b).
- `DOCUMENT_VALUES_SPEC.md` now defines `semantics` in the `prov.stream_end` row and gives one formula per semantics.
- `check_tail_unused` in `compare_anchors.py` must enforce the formula that matches the merged `semantics`. This change is routed to the fix round after the P1 code review.
- If the extracted semantics is `last_index`, the plan's 19,837 is off by one, and the correction goes to `interpretation_errata.md`.
