## Why it matters

P1:G2 requires the two extractions to agree. A paraphrase cannot be compared reliably between blind extractors, and a LaTeX tie is not a content difference.

## Decisive tests

- **The tie.** In LaTeX, `~` is a non-breaking space, so "Company~A" and "Company A" are the same title.
- **The unit of δ.** The predeclared δ is the half-width of the band on station shares (`CAMPAIGN_PREDECLARATION.md:440`, "two-sided rule, δ = 0.02"), which makes it a share. ν and λ are fractions.
- **The paraphrases.** The specification does not require verbatim text, so both readings satisfied it. That gap is closed by rule rather than by choosing one extractor.

## Resolution

Resolution: E, as follows.
1. **Text comparison.** Whitespace is normalised, and in LaTeX sources `~` counts as whitespace. `compare_anchors.py --documents` implements this in the P1 fix round. `comp.supp.sections` then agrees, and the merged value keeps the source spelling, "Company~A".
2. **Text values are verbatim.** A `text` value is copied from the cited source with its LaTeX kept, and is never paraphrased. `comp.supp.workload.definition` is re-extracted blind by both extractors under this rule, in the next read-only wave, and merged once they agree. The other text keys already agree and quote their source.
3. **Units.** `pred.holdout.delta` is a share; `pred.holdout.nu` and `pred.holdout.lambda` are fractions. The specification row is split to say so, and the merged file uses these units.
4. **Source of a merged value.** When A and B cite different valid lines for an agreed value, the merged entry cites the lower line in the same file, and the merge record notes the other line.
