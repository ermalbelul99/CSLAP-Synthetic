## 2. Deliverable repairs made during the run

### 2.1 `results.csv` overwrite (repaired)

`run_bs_robust_experiment.py:490` writes `results.csv` / `calibration.csv` /
`per_station.csv` with `to_csv` in overwrite mode, and `rows` starts empty per
process. The runbook grid loop (`RUNBOOK_daily_robustness.md:99`) sends all four
folds to the same `--out` per quantile, so each fold overwrote the previous and
only fold 3 would have survived.

Layout JSONs are fold-tagged and were never at risk, and `daily_metrics.py`
scores from layouts — so the *metrics* were unaffected. Only solver metadata
(bounds, gaps, timings, infeasibility records) was at risk.

Repair: per-fold snapshots were taken during the run and merged back. All three
quantile directories now hold the full 24 rows (4 folds × 6 arms), verified
row-by-row against the console logs.

### 2.2 One snapshot race (repaired, with a caveat)

The snapshot poller captured a 5-row intermediate state for `qp90` fold 3 and
missed `tight1.05`; the merge then overwrote the complete canonical file. The
missing arm was regenerated by re-solving it to a scratch directory
(`results/logs/step5_f3_qp90_recover_tight105.log`) and appending only that row.

**Caveat:** that row's `solve_time = 411.4` comes from the recovery run; the
original grid run recorded `377.7` for the same arm. The verdict (proven
infeasible) is identical in both. It is the only one of 72 rows whose value comes
from a different process. `results/_snapshots/berner_daily_qp90_f3_results.csv`
is still the truncated 5-row file, kept as-is for provenance.

### 2.3 `daily_metrics.py` layout collision (repaired — invalidated the first scoring)

`daily_metrics.py:98` recursively globs `results/**/layout_<fold_tag>*.json` and
keys arms by **filename only**, so the same arm name in different quantile
directories collides and the alphabetically-last path wins.

Running the issued command (`--results results`, `RUNBOOK:116`) therefore scored:

* folds 1–3, every arm → the **`qp95`** layout;
* fold 0 `g0`/`g1` → the **`daily_smoke`** layouts (the 120-second smoke test);
* the `qmax` and `qp90` layout sets contributed **zero** rows.

It also fabricated coverage: it reported a `g4` arm at `p90` for folds 1–3, where
no such layout exists (those arms were UNDETERMINED).

Repair: `daily_metrics.py` was invoked once per **training** quantile with
`--results` scoped to that directory, and rows tagged with a new leading column
`train_tcap_quantile`. `results/daily_metrics_berner.csv` now has 342 rows.
**Read the matched diagonal (`train_tcap_quantile == tcap_quantile`); the
off-diagonal rows are a layout trained at one ceiling and scored at another.**

## 4. Findings that limit what these results can support

These are properties of the pipeline as written. **Nothing here was changed** —
they are reported so the numbers are not over-read.

1. **CPLEX did not optimise anything.** In all 46 cells that returned an
   incumbent, the objective is bit-identical to the pre-solve warm-start
   objective. The 120 s smoke layout is byte-identical to the 800 s grid layout
   (md5 `92c6904a…`). The arms are *incumbent + ~135 s local search*, not MILP
   optima.

2. **The reported dual gap is not a quality measure.** `solver_bound` equals the
   trivial "every multi-item order costs at least one visit" bound Σ_o w_o
   exactly — fold 1 = 13423.0, fold 2 = 14118.0, fold 3 = 14776.0, matching the
   CPLEX bound to the decimal (fold 0 within 0.9). The "27–34% gap" is
   warm-start ÷ do-nothing bound.

3. **The returned solutions are provably suboptimal.** The Γ-feasible set shrinks
   as Γ grows, so the optimum must be non-decreasing in Γ. **8 of 12** fold×q
   cells violate this (e.g. q=max fold 2: 19366 → 19375 → **19239** → 19413).

4. **Γ-monotonicity does not hold at matched ceilings.** Violated-day counts are
   flat in Γ (10, 10, 11, 10 at q=max). Γ does monotonically reduce *severity*
   (worst ratio 2.241 → 2.149; mean ratio 1.099 → 1.035). An earlier apparent
   monotone trend in the count was an artefact of §2.3.

5. **The β arm does not use the revealed ceiling, so the 24 proven
   infeasibilities are not evidence about revealed capacity.**
   `run_bs_robust_experiment.py:663` computes one flat ceiling from
   `stations[0]["SPEED"]` for all stations. BERNER speeds span **1677×**
   (6.20 → 10400); the revealed q=max ceilings span 0.0151 → 87.06, yet every
   station is given a flat **13**. `t_beta` is independent of `--tcap-quantile`
   (identical 13/14 at q=max and q=p90), so the 24 β cells are ~5 distinct MILPs
   re-solved. **A4 is therefore untested**, and `RUNBOOK:138-140`'s reading of β
   infeasibility as "the drift does not fit inside the revealed capacity" does
   not follow.

6. **The optimised layouts break the daily ceiling on the days they were trained
   on.** `daily_folds.py` sets `REAL_LINES` to a per-day **mean** while `T_s` is
   the **max** daily load, so the MILP row is a mean-day constraint checked
   against a max-day ceiling. Train-window violated days at q=max:

   | fold | days | incumbent | g0 | g1 | g2 | g4 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0 | 38 | **0** | 35 | 31 | 28 | 28 |
   | 1 | 43 | **0** | 30 | 27 | 29 | 25 |
   | 2 | 47 | **0** | 30 | 25 | 25 | 25 |
   | 3 | 51 | **0** | 29 | 29 | 29 | 28 |

   That is 53–92% of their own training days. The test-window result is the
   continuation of an in-sample failure, not out-of-sample generalisation loss.
   (Note `wl_broken_asrun` in `results.csv` is a **test-window** count, 19–24 of
   24 stations — it does not report this train-window breakage.)

7. **Fold 0 is out of envelope under A3.** It trains 2021-09-01 → 2021-10-22 and
   tests 2021-10-25 → 2021-10-29, and its test window contains 2021-10-29 — the
   horizon peak (`diagnostics_berner.json`, `peak_date`). All five headline
   incumbent violations are fold 0; folds 1–3 are 0/13 at q=max. A3 declares
   surges beyond the observed envelope out of scope, so fold 0 should be reported
   as a deliberate stress case, not pooled.

8. **The incumbent train contract is an algebraic identity, not evidence.** With
   `T_s = max_d W_s(d)` under the incumbent, `worst_day_ratio == 1.000` and
   `days_violated == 0` follow by construction. It is a useful code-correctness
   assertion (and it passed on all four folds) but carries no information about
   A2.

9. **The optimiser cannot change station occupancy.** `sum(CAPACITY) == 2000 ==
   n_products` exactly on every fold, so with Σ_s x_ps = 1 the slot constraint is
   tight everywhere: every feasible layout is a permutation of the incumbent's
   free-SKU occupancy vector.

10. **`viol_norm`, `max_ratio_norm`, `X`, `imbalance` are unit-mismatched.** They
    compare a test-window total against a now-*daily* ceiling, giving
    `viol_norm = 24/24` and ratios of 40–63 in every row. They predate the daily
    conversion; do not interpret them.

11. **A1 is assumed, not tested.** No window-vs-day comparison exists in
    `Baselines/`; daily-ness is imposed in fold construction.

## 5. Synthetic arm — essentially absent

`results/daily_metrics_exp02a.csv` covers **one instance, one fold, 5 test days**
(`syn_500sku_seed1001_d00_r0f0`) — a timing probe, **not** the synthetic study.
The issued step list contained only the 12 industrial runs; the runbook's separate
synthetic grid (`RUNBOOK:105`) was not part of it. Measured cost: **27 m 21 s per
fold directory × 112 directories ≈ 50 h**, because the ~128 s warm start and the
full 180 s limit are incurred for all five configs regardless of instance size.
The `_d15` drift level was never built.

One substantive contrast is visible even from the probe: on the **homogeneous**
synthetic instance (all speeds 1.0) the identical β code produces a **feasible**
`tight1.02` arm, which supports §4.5 — the industrial β infeasibility is driven by
the speed-heterogeneity bug, not by demand.
